Frequently asked questions

How much does product photography cost in Columbus, Ohio?

Most Columbus product photographers charge between $25 and $95 per product photo for straightforward catalog shots on white, with bundles bringing the per-image price down as volume goes up. A small batch (5–10 products) typically runs $150–$500; a full catalog shoot with styling and multiple angles runs higher. Always ask what the price includes: retouching, background variants, and usage rights are the three items that move quotes apart.

Do I need a professional photographer, or can I shoot products myself?

You can shoot clean catalog photos yourself with a phone, a $40 light box, and consistent daylight — good enough for marketplace listings and proof-of-concept stores. Professional photography earns its cost where the photo has to sell by itself: hero images, ads, and premium product pages. A practical split many Columbus stores use: self-shot catalog images, professionally shot hero and ad images.

What makes a product photo convert well on a store listing?

Three things, in order: the product fills the frame and is sharp edge-to-edge; the background stays consistent across the whole catalog; and the listing includes at least one real-use or scale shot. Buyers decide in seconds — a crisp, consistent gallery reads as a trustworthy seller, and inconsistent galleries read as a drop-shipper who never touched the product.

How many photos should each product listing have?

Five to eight: the hero (front, white background), a 45-degree angle, a detail/macro on material or stitching, a scale shot (in a hand or on a surface), one in-use photo, and one lifestyle photo where it makes sense. eBay and Shopify both reward complete galleries with better placement and conversion.

How fast can a Columbus photographer turn photos around?

Typical turnaround for a catalog batch is 3–7 business days from shoot to edited files. Rush turnaround (24–48 hours) is common for launch dates but usually costs a premium. If a photographer promises same-day edited batch work, ask how much of the retouching is automated — automated retouch is fine for white-background catalog shots but shows on detail work.

How to choose a Columbus product photographer

Ask for three things before you book anyone:

1. A catalog they shot, not a portfolio. Portfolios show the best ten photos from years of work. A catalog shows what your whole product line will actually look like — consistent lighting, consistent angles, consistent retouching across dozens of SKUs. Consistency is what you are paying for.

2. The exact deliverables. How many final images per product, at what resolution, in what formats (web-ready JPG plus high-res master), with what usage rights, and what happens if a shot needs a re-shoot. A photographer who answers this precisely has done catalog work before.

3. A real timeline. Shoot date, delivery date, and how revisions work. A photographer managing five other clients will be honest about a two-week window; one who promises everything immediately is telling you what you want to hear.
